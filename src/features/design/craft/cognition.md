**Group with a deliberate signal (OPINION).** Gestalt research (Wertheimer; Palmer; Palmer &
Rock) supports similarity, common region and connectedness as grouping signals. Give related
operations a shared region or toolbar and equivalent controls the same treatment; reserve
enclosure when space cannot carry the relationship. Let `spacing` choose distances; do not box
every section.

**Make one focal exception (OPINION).** Broadbent's selective-attention account and von
Restorff's distinctiveness result support one task-relevant action or recommendation as the
visual exception. If every option gets a badge or equal emphasis, nothing wins. Pair appearance
with position or wording; conformance belongs to `accessibility`.

**Weight choices before hiding them (OPINION).** Hick and Hyman found decision time grows with
equivalent alternatives; Iyengar and Lepper documented choice overload. Expose primary paths,
mark one recommended choice when it suits most people, and put secondary paths behind explicit
“More”. Never delete a needed path to hit an arbitrary count.

**Let recognition carry memory (OPINION).** Working-memory research makes prior context costly
to recreate. Persist filters, selections, comparison context and breadcrumbs across steps; label
sections so people recognise where they are instead of memorising prior choices.

**Show honest progress (OPINION).** Goal-gradient and Zeigarnik findings support progress for
genuinely multi-step work: show completed prerequisites and the current step. Never fabricate
completion, streaks or urgency; progress must describe work that exists.

**Plan for acquisition (OPINION).** Fitts predicts that aiming cost changes with distance and
target size. Place frequent actions in the task's natural hand and eye path; use `spacing` for
distance and `accessibility` or `icons` for target size. It does not license a universal pixel
rule.

**What is contested.** “Miller's 7±2” is not a menu limit: Miller's paper concerns channel
capacity for chunks, and Cowan's bound is context-dependent. Hick does not justify a universal
3–5 cap; Fitts predicts speed–accuracy, not a target-size or spacing requirement. Gestalt laws
describe tendencies, not a prescription for cards or a fixed grid. Numeric floors live in
`spacing`, `accessibility`, and `icons`.
