Generated decks tend to shrink a document onto slides: full paragraphs, sentences as bullets, and charts pasted at a size no seat in the room can read.

**State one claim per slide (OPINION).** Give each slide a headline that says its point, then at most a few short support lines. Sentences belong in speaker notes, never as bullets. A common six-lines-of-six-words rule of thumb is a budget, not a law; fewer, shorter lines always survive. Number every slide: the count orients the room when attention drifts.

**Compose inside the frame (CONVENTION).** A slide is a fixed 16:9 box, not a scrolling column: keep the headline, body, and slide number inside the margins and clear of the edges, and preview the deck at the size it will be shown ([Apple Layout HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)). A slide that needs scrolling in the browser needs squinting in the room. Repeat the frame from slide to slide: same margins, same headline position, same footer. The room reads position before text, so a headline that moves is a hierarchy that restarts. When one slide overflows, split it in two instead of shrinking the type: density is a cut, not a scale.

**Keep off the slide what the room cannot read (OPINION).** Paragraphs, data tables beyond a handful of cells, screenshots of text, and legends that need a pointer do not belong on a slide. One chart per slide, with its takeaway in the headline; hand the detail out as a document instead of shrinking it. A background photograph stays decoration only: dim it behind the text or drop it, never set type over a busy image at full strength.

**Set type to survive the back row (CONVENTION; WCAG contrast STANDARD).** Hold body text to SC 1.4.3 (AA): 4.5:1 against its background, 3:1 for large text of at least 18 pt or 14 pt bold ([WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum)). No universal minimum point size is sourced, so verify legibility at the shown size instead of trusting a number. Keep lines short enough to take in at a glance; a line that wraps three times is a paragraph wearing a bullet.

**What is contested.** Exact line maxima differ across guides, so carry the purpose, not a fixed count: one idea per slide, readable at distance, with the evidence spoken or handed out. A known pitch formula fixes ten slides in twenty minutes; that count serves a pitch, not every deck, so take the count from the occasion instead.
