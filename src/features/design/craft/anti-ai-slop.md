These are the defaults to break rather than inherit: a model reaches for them when nothing
in the brief pushes back.

**Indigo and violet.** `#6366f1`, `#8b5cf6`, `#7c3aed` are the most reliable tell: the
training data is saturated with them, so they are what "average" looks like. Use them when
the brief or the project's own tokens ask for them, not otherwise.

**Cards as the default container.** Border, shadow, radius and background around anything
that groups. A card is earned when the container is the thing being interacted with. If
removing all four hurts neither interaction nor comprehension, it was never a card.

**Dark by default.** Dark is a brand decision. When the brief says nothing about mode, light.

**Calm editorial autopilot.** Ivory ground, oversized high-contrast serif, one word
italicized in a second face, muted clay or olive accents. Right for a magazine, a hotel, a
fashion house. Slop on a dashboard or a developer tool, because it was picked for taste
rather than for the product.

**Emoji standing in for icons.** Rocket, sparkle and target glyphs in headings, buttons and
list markers. A monoline SVG using `currentColor`, a Unicode arrow, or a plain label all
read as finished; the emoji reads as unfinished.

**A colored stripe down one edge of a card.** In shipped products that stripe carries
meaning — status, priority, selection. If you cannot say in one word what it means, it is
decoration.

**Numbers nobody measured.** "10x faster", "99.9% uptime". Likewise filler: lorem ipsum,
"Feature one / two / three". An empty section is a composition problem; inventing words does
not solve it.

**Imagery faked in CSS.** Blobs, gradient meshes and abstract shapes standing in for a
photograph. When the asset is unavailable, use a deliberate placeholder — correct aspect
ratio, a caption naming what belongs there — and keep the space it needs.

The test: hide the logo. If the first screen could belong to any other product, no choice
has been made yet.
